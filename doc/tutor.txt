= The stack
MM-41 is a Reverse Polish calculator: you put numbers on a stack and then say what to
do with them. There is no = key and there are never any parentheses. 3 + 4 is typed as
3, then 4, then +. Type the line below and press Enter. The calculator shows the stack
after every line, and the tutor then says whether the lesson's expectation holds.
> 3 4 +
? 7

The 7 is still on the stack. Every result stays there, ready for the next operation, so
a calculation is a chain: type 3 * to multiply the 7 by 3.
> 3 *
? 21

Longer expressions need no parentheses because the stack remembers the intermediate
results. (2 + 3) * (4 + 5) is: 2 3 + makes 5, 4 5 + makes 9, * multiplies them.
> 2 3 + 4 5 + *
? 45

The listing shows the stack from the bottom up: [0] is the oldest item and the last line
is the top, the most recent one. Right now there are two items, 21 and 45. dup copies
the top, drop removes it, swap exchanges the top two. Type dup to copy the 45.
> dup
? depth 3

Now there are three items. Remove the copy again with drop, and then swap the 21 and
the 45.
> drop swap
? 21

A subtraction or division takes the second item minus (or divided by) the top, in the
order you typed them: 10 4 - is 6. Here the stack holds 45 and 21, so - gives 24.
> -
? 24

A wrong line is never fatal: undo restores the stack as it was before the last line,
and clst clears it entirely. Clear the stack now.
> clst
? empty

That is all there is to RPN: numbers go on the stack, commands take their arguments
from it and leave their result on it. Other stack words you will meet: over (copy the
second item to the top), roll and pick (reach deeper), depth (how many items). help
Stack lists them all.
? any

= Numbers and functions
Functions work the same way as +: the argument goes first. pi pushes pi, sin takes the
sine (in radians) of the top. Two inverted commas make a string, (1,2) a complex
number, 0.1d a decimal; all three live on the same stack as numbers.
> pi 2 / sin
? 1

Powers, roots and logarithms: 2 10 ^ is 2 to the tenth, sqrt, exp, ln and log (base 10)
take the top. Compute 2^10.
> 2 10 ^
? 1024

The display shows six decimals by default. 10 setprec shows ten; sfs toggles fixed and
scientific notation. Changing the display changes nothing about the numbers. Set the
display to 10 places and look at pi.
> 10 setprec pi
? 3.141592653589793

Back to 6 places. The percent words keep their base on the stack, as HP calculators
do: 200 25 % leaves 200 and 50 -- the base and 25 percent of it. %chg gives the percent
change from the second item to the top.
> 6 setprec clst 80 100 %chg
? 25

Combinatorics: fact, ncr (combinations), npr (permutations). How many five-card hands
does a deck of 52 allow?
> clst 52 5 ncr
? 2598960

A negative number has a complex square root, and the calculator says so instead of
complaining: the result below is (0,2), the complex number 2i. Complex numbers are typed
the same way, (3,4), and abs, arg, re, im and conj work on them.
> -4 sqrt
? complex

Money wants decimals. In decimal mode every number you type is an exact decimal with 34
digits and half-even rounding, so 0.1 + 0.2 is exactly 0.3, which binary doubles
cannot say. The prompt changes to MM-41[dec]>> while the mode is on.
> clst decimal 0.1 0.2 +
? 0.3

The finance words always compute in decimals, whatever the mode. Everything else --
matrices, the numerical methods -- is binary, and >bin converts a decimal when you need
one there. Leave decimal mode with binary.
> binary clst
? empty

help <name> describes any command with its stack picture and an example, which it also
runs: try help ncr. help alone is the quick-start screen, and help <text> searches:
help bessel finds the Bessel functions, help percent the percent words.
? any

= Your own words
A word is a command you define, Forth style: a colon, the name, the body, a semicolon,
all on one line. Once defined it is used like any built-in. Define hyp, the hypotenuse
of the two numbers on the stack: square each, add, take the root.
> : hyp dup * swap dup * + sqrt ;
? word hyp

Now use it.
> 3 4 hyp
? 5

The body may use the stack freely and call other words, including itself. A recursive
factorial: if the top is greater than 1, multiply by the factorial of one less.
> : fac dup 1 gt if dup 1 - fac * then ;
? word fac

Twenty factorial.
> clst 20 fac
? 2432902008176640000

if ... then and if ... else ... then test the top of the stack (0 is false), and
begin ... until, begin ... while ... repeat and limit start do ... loop give you
loops with i as the index. This word sums the integers from 1 to n with a do loop.
> : sumto 0 swap 1 + 1 do i + loop ;
? word sumto

The sum of 1 to 100.
> clst 100 sumto
? 5050

A word that merely names a constant is a word too, and a handy one: the const module
shipped with MM-41 is nothing but such words (c, h, G, kB, qe, NA ...), and the std
module has sq, cube, pctchg, f2c, c2f and friends. listmacros shows the words that
came from modules, listwords your own.
> listwords
? any

Your words vanish when you quit unless you save them: savewords writes them to
user_words.txt in the config directory, which is loaded at startup. "hyp" delword
removes one, clrwords all of them. A whole family of related words belongs in a word
module: a text file name.mm in ~/.config/mm41/modules/, one word per line, loaded with
use name. help modules explains the format.
? any

= Registers and strings
There are 64 registers, numbered 0 to 63, which hold anything a stack item can hold.
x n sto stores x in register n; n rcl pushes a copy. Store 2.5 in register 0.
> clst 2.5 0 sto
? empty

Recall it, square it.
> 0 rcl dup *
? 6.25

Registers are how a word gets at a value without having it passed on the stack. This
word uses whatever is in register 0 as a scale factor.
> : scale 0 rcl * ;
? word scale

10 scaled by the 2.5 in register 0.
> clst 10 scale
? 25

The std module has sto_add, sto_sub, sto_mul, sto_div: 1 0 sto_add adds 1 to register
0. pr shows which registers are occupied, clregs clears them all, saveregs and loadregs
keep them across sessions.
> 1 0 sto_add 0 rcl
? 3.5

Strings are items too. scon concatenates, slen measures, s2u and s2l change case, substr
cuts, int2str makes a string from a number. Strings name files for readmat, batch and
the other file words, and they are what the date words work with ("1.1.2025").
> clst "MM-" "41" scon
? "MM-41"

A string can also be evaluated: "1 2 +" eval runs it as a line of input. That is the
loophole through which a word can build and run code, and through which a line of a
file becomes a calculation.
> clst "6 7 *" eval
? 42

= Matrices
A matrix is one item on the stack. [1 2 3] is a row vector, [1 2; 3 4] a 2 x 2 matrix
(a semicolon ends a row). The arithmetic words work on them: * is the matrix product, a
scalar is applied elementwise, .* ./ .^ are the elementwise operations. Enter a matrix.
> clst [4 7; 2 6]
? matrix 2x2

det takes the determinant (and drops the matrix); dup first to keep a copy.
> dup det
? 10

Drop the determinant and invert the matrix with minv. The listing shows a matrix by
its size; pm prints it in full.
> drop minv pm
? matrix 2x2

A linear system A x = b is solved with the inverse. With A = [4 7; 2 6] and b the
column [1; 1], A^-1 b is [-0.1; 0.3]. (The inverse is already on the stack; put b on
top and multiply.)
> [1; 1] *
? matrix 2x1

Reductions work by column or by row: csum cmean cvar cmin cmax (a row of results),
rsum rmean ... (a column). For a vector, mean, sd, median and quantile return a number.
> clst [1 2 3 4 10] mean
? 4

rrange builds a row vector from start, step, end; eye, ones, zeroes, rand and randn
build matrices; reshape, tran (or '), join_h, join_v, getcol, getrow, get_aij and
set_aij rearrange them. 0 to 1 in steps of 0.25:
> clst 0 0.25 1 rrange
? matrix 1x5

Polynomials are vectors of coefficients, highest power first. roots finds all the roots
(as a complex row vector), pval evaluates. The roots of x^2 - 3x + 2:
> clst [1 -3 2] roots
? matrix 1x2

eig, svd, chol, pinv, kron and the rest are in help matrices. A matrix can also come
from a file: "table.txt" readmat reads one row per line, numbers separated by spaces,
tabs or commas, skipping comments and a header line -- chapter 14 shows it.
? any

= Numerical methods
Any word can be handed to a numerical method as a function: @name pushes a reference
to it, an execution token, instead of running it. a b @f integrate integrates f over
[a, b]. The integral of the sine over [0, pi]:
> clst 0 pi @sin integrate
? 2

The function is usually a word of your own, which takes x from the stack and leaves
f(x). Define f(x) = x^2 - 2 ...
> : f dup * 2 - ;
? word f

... and find its root in the bracket [0, 3] with fzero (Brent's method), which is the
square root of 2.
> clst 0 3 @f fzero
? 1.4142135624

solve needs only a guess; minimize finds the argument of the minimum in an interval;
deriv differentiates at a point; fsum and fprod sum or multiply f(i) over the integers.
The sum of 1/i^2 from 1 to 1000 is close to pi^2/6:
> : invsq dup * inv ;
? word invsq

> clst 1 1000 @invsq fsum
? 1.643934567

A function with a parameter -- a Bessel function of order n, sin kx -- is a word that
fixes the parameter. besselj is x n -- Jn(x); this word supplies n = 2:
> : j2 2 besselj ;
? word j2

The integral of J2 over [0, 10].
> clst 0 10 @j2 integrate
? 0.980066

Keep the parameter in a register instead and one word serves every value: : jn 0 rcl
besselj ; then 2 0 sto, 3 0 sto ... Built-ins and module words take @ as well
(@sin, @npdf). x tol sets the tolerance (default 1e-10), nevals tells how many function
evaluations the last call took. help numerics has the summary.
> nevals
? any

= Finance
The finance module is the HP-12C's financial registers: n i% pv pmt fv and, for
payments that grow at a constant rate, g%. Set the ones you know, ask for the one you
do not with ?n ?i% ?pv ?pmt ?fv ?g%. Cash received is positive, cash paid negative.
A 30-year mortgage of 300,000 at 6% a year: 360 monthly periods at 0.5%, fv 0.
> clst clrtvm 360 n 0.5 i% 300000 pv 0 fv ?pmt
? -1798.651541

The payment is a decimal (the finance words compute in decimals), and it is now in the
pmt register. How long would 2,000 a month take? Store it and solve for n.
> clst -2000 pmt ?n
? 277.9514

tvm shows the registers; amort prints the schedule, amortm leaves it as a matrix of
payment, interest, principal and balance per period. beg makes payments fall at the
beginning of each period. Show the registers.
> tvm
? any

A payment growing at g% per period: a pension of 1,000 a month growing 0.25% a month
(3% a year) for 20 years at 0.4% a month. Its present value:
> clst clrtvm 240 n 0.4 i% -1000 pmt 0.25 g% 0 fv ?pv
? 201006.41

Cash flows: a column vector in time order, the first at t = 0 (or a matrix of amount
and repeat count), npv at a rate in percent, irr for the internal rate of return.
> clst clrtvm [-1000; 300; 400; 500] irr
? 8.896339

Conversions and more: nom>eff and eff>nom between nominal and effective rates, sl soyd
db for depreciation, bprice byield for bonds on a coupon date and xnpv xirr xprice
xyield for dated cash flows and bonds (Excel's conventions). 12% nominal, monthly:
> clst 12 12 nom>eff
? 12.6825

= Dates and workdays
Dates are strings d.m.yyyy. ddays counts the days between two, dateplus adds days, dow
names the weekday, today is today. Days from New Year to Christmas 2026:
> clst "1.1.2026" "25.12.2026" ddays
? 358

> clst "25.12.2026" dow
? "Friday"

Workdays follow Excel's conventions: d1 d2 workdays counts Mondays to Fridays from d1
to d2 inclusive, less the holidays in the installed calendar; date n workdayplus steps
n workdays ahead. The calendar is a vector of dates YYYYMMDD; sethol installs one for
this session, usehol us (or si, or your own file in holidays/) installs one and
remembers it. Install Thanksgiving and Christmas 2026 (a calendar you had installed is
back at the next start, or now with usehol), then count the workdays of December.
> clst [20261126 20261225] sethol "1.12.2026" "31.12.2026" workdays
? 22

20 workdays after 1 December 2026, skipping Christmas:
> clst "1.12.2026" 20 workdayplus
? "30.12.2026"

The finance words take dates as the number YYYYMMDD; date>ymd and ymd>date convert.
sunrise, sunset, dawn and dusk take a date, latitude, longitude and a time zone (an
IANA name or an offset in hours); the std module has a few places ready: today
alexandria sunset. Sunset in Kranj at midsummer:
> clst "21.6.2026" kranj sunset
? string

= Statistics and simulation
Vectors are data. mean, sd, sem, median, quantile, skew, kurt describe one; cov and corr
relate two; linfit, expfit, logfit and powfit fit a curve and return [a, b, R^2]; ols
and olstab regress on several columns. A least-squares line through four points:
> clst [1 2 3 4] [2 4 5 9] linfit
? matrix 1x3

The row on the stack holds intercept, slope and R-squared; split_mat takes it apart into
three numbers, A i j get_aij copies one element (0-based). The slope:
> 0 1 get_aij
? 2.2

Distributions: ncdf nquant npdf for the normal, tcdf tinv, chi2cdf chi2inv, fcdf finv,
binompdf binomcdf, poispdf poiscdf. The two-sided 95% critical value of Student's t
with 10 degrees of freedom:
> clst 0.975 10 tinv
? 2.228139

Random draws: n mu sigma normrnd makes n normal draws as a column (also unifrnd poisrnd
binomrnd exprnd trnd chi2rnd), rnorm and runif make one. seed makes a run reproducible.
The mean of 1000 standard normals:
> clst 42 seed 1000 0 1 normrnd mean
? near 0 0.15

Monte Carlo over a word: n @w sims runs w n times and collects the number it leaves
each time; mcstats summarises the result as mean, sd and the 5th, 50th and 95th
percentiles. A game: the sum of two dice.
> : dice 1 7 runif intg 1 7 runif intg + ;
? word dice

> clst 10000 @dice sims mcstats
? matrix 1x5

A histogram of the same draws: v k hist tabulates k bins, v k hplot draws them (chapter
13). resample draws a bootstrap sample, shuffle permutes.
> clst 10000 @dice sims 11 hist
? matrix 11x2

= Differential equations
odeint integrates y' = f(t, y) from t0 to t1 with an adaptive Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg
method. The right-hand side is a word that takes t and y and leaves y'. For y' = -y
the word drops t and changes the sign of y:
> : decay swap drop chs ;
? word decay

y0 t0 t1 @f odesol gives the state at t1 only. From y(0) = 1 to t = 1: e^-1.
> clst 1 0 1 @decay odesol
? 0.367879

y0 t0 t1 n @f odeint leaves the trajectory at n + 1 equally spaced times, t in the
first column and the state in the rest. The logistic equation y' = y (1 - y) from
y(0) = 0.1 over [0, 10] in 20 steps:
> : logistic swap drop dup 1 swap - * ;
? word logistic

> clst 0.1 0 10 20 @logistic odeint
? matrix 21x2

A system is a row vector state: the word takes t and the vector and leaves the vector
of derivatives. mplot draws every column of the trajectory against the first (chapter
13); getcol takes a column out, get_aij one element. The last value of y, row 20,
column 1, has all but reached the carrying capacity:
> 20 1 get_aij
? near 1 0.001

= Survival analysis
The survival module works from a lifetime law chosen once with setlaw: exponential,
weibull, gompertz, makeham, siler, loglogistic, lognormal or gamma with its parameters.
Gompertz with b = 0.0001 and c = 0.09 is a fair adult mortality:
> clst "gompertz" [0.0001 0.09] setlaw
? empty

surv is the survival function, haz the hazard, lifepdf, lifecdf and lifeinv the density,
distribution and quantile. The probability of reaching 65:
> 65 surv
? 0.680651

elife is the complete expectation of remaining life at an age, lifemean the expectation
at birth, lifemed the median, lifemode the modal age at death (ln(c/b)/c for Gompertz).
Remaining life at 65:
> clst 65 elife
? 11.876902

Actuarial words: x n tpx and nqx (survive or die within n years), px qx lx, x0 x1
lifetab (a life table with lx, dx, qx and ex per age), and the present values x i ax
(whole-life annuity-due at i%), axn, Ax (whole-life insurance), Axn. The value of 1 a
year for life from 65 at 3%:
> clst 65 3 ax
? 10.014379

A life table from 60 to 70:
> clst 60 70 lifetab
? matrix 11x5

Data: t e km is the Kaplan-Meier estimate from times and event indicators (1 event,
0 censored), nelsonaalen the cumulative hazard, logrank compares two groups, lifefit
fits the current law by maximum likelihood, lawfit and lawfitl fit it to a published
life table's qx or lx. help survival has the overview, manual survival the chapter.
> clst [2 3 3 5 8 9] [1 1 0 1 0 1] km
? matrix

= Income distribution
The income module does the same for the distribution of income: setdist picks a family
(pareto, lognormal, dagum, singhmaddala, fisk, gamma, weibull) and its parameters. A
Dagum distribution with a = 3.5, b = 20000, p = 0.8:
> clst "dagum" [3.5 20000 0.8] setdist
? empty

gini, theil, mld and eps atkinson measure inequality in closed form or by quadrature;
incmean incvar incmed incmode describe the distribution; incpdf inccdf incq evaluate it.
> gini
? 0.302546

The income below which 90% of the population lies, and the share of the top 10%:
> clst 0.9 incq
? 35019.96

> clst 0.1 top
? 0.238405

deciles gives the ten decile shares as a row, palma the Palma ratio, p1 p2 share the
share between two quantiles, z headcount, z povgap and z alpha fgt the poverty
measures at a poverty line z. The share living on less than 10,000:
> clst 10000 headcount
? 0.134180

Fitting: v incfit fits the current family to a vector of incomes by maximum likelihood,
v incfits to the quintile or decile shares of a published table, p L incfitl to points
of a Lorenz curve. The data words ginid theild lorenzd decilesd topd palmad headcountd
povgapd fgtd work directly on a vector of incomes:
> clst [1 2 3 4 10] ginid
? 0.4

= Graphs
The plot module draws in the terminal, two pixels to a character cell, with round-number
ticks. a b @f fplot draws a word over an interval:
> clst 0 10 @sin fplot
? any

hold keeps the figure, so the next plot is added; plegend names the series and show
redraws with the legend.
> hold 0 10 @cos fplot "sin,cos" plegend show
? any

x y plot draws data as a line, scatter as points, steps as a step function (a Kaplan-
Meier curve), bars as bars, v k hplot a histogram, M mplot every column of a matrix
against the first (an odeint trajectory, a Lorenz curve, a life table). ptitle,
pxlabel and pylabel label it; pxlog and pylog make an axis logarithmic; pxrange,
pyrange and psize fix the ranges and the size. A histogram of 2000 normal draws:
> nohold clst 2000 0 1 normrnd 25 hplot
? any

"fig.svg" psvg writes the figure as vector graphics for a document, "fig.txt" ptxt as
text. pclear forgets the figure and its settings.
> pclear
? any

= Files, scripts and the shell
A line starting with ! runs a shell command and pushes what it printed as a string.
Write a small table to a file this way (the command prints nothing, so the string is
empty):
> !printf '1 2\n3 4\n5 6\n' > /tmp/mm41-tutor.txt
? string

"file" readmat reads a table, one row per line, numbers separated by spaces, tabs,
commas or semicolons; readvec reads every number as one column; "file" j readcol one
column. Blank lines, comments and a header line are skipped.
> clst "/tmp/mm41-tutor.txt" readmat
? matrix 3x2

"file" batch runs the lines of a file as if typed. From the shell, mm41 -e 'line' runs
one line, mm41 file args... runs a script (so #!/usr/bin/env mm41 makes one a
program), the arguments pushed first; the word . prints the top plainly and n exit
sets the exit status. The exit status is 1 if any error was reported, which lets a
script live in a Makefile or a cron job. help syntax and manual Scripts have the
details.
? any

savewords, saveregs and savestack keep words, registers and the stack between sessions;
the display settings, number mode, tolerance, holiday calendar, skin and this tutor's
progress are kept in config.txt in ~/.config/mm41/.
? any

= Finding your way
help <name> describes a command: its stack picture, what each item means, an example
that is run for you, and the related commands. Try it on %chg.
> help %chg
? any

help <module> lists a module's commands, help <category> a category, help <text>
searches names and descriptions, and help <topic> explains one subject: finance,
numerics, survival, income, plot, words, syntax, modules. TAB completes all of them.
> help finance
? any

manual opens the user manual in the pager; manual <chapter> or manual <word> opens it
at the right place (n finds the next match, q returns). The manual has the long
explanations and the worked examples that help leaves out; the PDF is in doc/.
? any

listfcns lists every command by category, modules the loaded modules with their
versions, listmacros the words that came from word modules, "name" usage a word's
definition.
> modules
? any

The stack can be shown as a classic calculator would show it: skin hp41, skin hp12c,
skin hp48, skin hp48px; skin plain returns to the listing. The choice is remembered.
Try the HP-41 and come back.
> skin hp41
? any

> skin plain
? any

That is the tour. examples <chapter> prints any chapter of this book with every line and
its result, tutor <chapter> replays one, and the manual goes deeper on each subject.
The rest is practice.
? any
